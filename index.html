How it works

Welcome to Text-o-Matic

Text-o-Matic turns a list of phone numbers into a tap-through queue of personalized text messages. Write your message once, add names or other details with merge fields, and Text-o-Matic prepares each message for you. No more copying and pasting the same message over and over again.

  1. Add your list by pasting phone numbers or uploading a CSV or Excel workbook.
  2. Choose the phone column, the name shown for each recipient, merge fields, and ignored columns.
  3. Write a message using fields like {{First Name}}.
  4. Review missing data and customize fallback messages when needed.
  5. Send with SMS or WhatsApp on this device, or transfer the prepared list and messaging-app choice directly to another device.
Private by design — and you can verify it.

Your contacts, uploaded file contents, phone numbers, merge-field values, and message text are processed locally in your browser. Text-o-Matic does not upload that information to a Text-o-Matic server or send it to Cloudflare Web Analytics. Unfinished queues can be stored temporarily in this browser so they can be resumed.

Don't take my word for it: once Text-o-Matic says Offline ready, disconnect from the internet and keep using it; inspect the open-source code; or watch your browser's network traffic while you use the app.

Privacy

How Text-o-Matic handles your data

Text-o-Matic does not upload your contact lists or message text. Uploaded file contents, phone numbers, names, merge fields, contact-card fields, and prepared messages are processed locally in your browser. That information is not sent to a Text-o-Matic server or to Cloudflare Web Analytics.

You can verify this yourself. Once the app reports Offline ready, it can keep preparing lists and messages with your internet connection turned off. You can also inspect its network traffic while you work or read the source code on GitHub.

Unfinished queues are stored temporarily so they can be resumed. Once texting begins, Text-o-Matic stores the prepared recipients, messages, queue position, statuses, and any mapped contact-card fields in IndexedDB in this browser. When every recipient has been messaged or skipped, that persistent resume copy is removed immediately. Choosing Discard & erase also removes it immediately. If a queue is left unfinished, it expires after 7 days of inactivity and is removed the next time Text-o-Matic runs. If Text-o-Matic is never opened again, the app cannot wake itself up later just to delete browser storage.

Device Transfer does not upload your list. Text-o-Matic places the transfer payload directly in one or more QR codes rather than storing a server-side copy. See exactly how this works.

Device Transfers are encrypted by default. In encrypted mode, Text-o-Matic encrypts the prepared transfer locally using AES-GCM before creating the QR codes. The QR codes contain encrypted data, while the separate six-word transfer code needed to decrypt them is shown only on the first device and is not included in the QR codes. The transfer code stays hidden on the QR screen until you choose to reveal it after scanning. Encryption can be turned off for an easier transfer; in that case the QR codes contain readable contact and message data.

Temporary Device Transfer data is also cleaned up. While a multi-code transfer is being scanned, received parts are stored temporarily in IndexedDB so the next QR code can be added. Encrypted transfers remain encrypted in that temporary storage until the separate transfer code is entered. The temporary transfer record is removed when the list becomes the active texting queue. Abandoned or incomplete transfers expire after 24 hours of inactivity and are removed the next time Text-o-Matic runs.

Data outside Text-o-Matic is outside its control. A camera or QR-scanning app may keep its own scan history depending on its settings. With the default encrypted transfer, that history should contain encrypted transfer data rather than readable contacts or messages; with encryption turned off, it may contain the readable QR payload. Once a message is opened in your SMS app or WhatsApp, it is subject to that app's, service's, and where applicable your carrier's privacy, security, and retention practices. Text-o-Matic cannot view or delete information stored by those other apps or services.

Your browser and device have their own privacy behavior. Browser privacy settings, private/incognito modes, storage-management policies, or clearing site data may delete Text-o-Matic's local storage earlier than described above, which can make an unfinished queue impossible to resume. Depending on your browser, operating system, backup settings, sync features, or device-management configuration, locally stored browser data may also be handled by features outside Text-o-Matic's control. On a shared device or browser profile, another person with access to that profile may be able to reopen an unfinished queue.

Closing an unfinished queue does not automatically erase it. Text-o-Matic uses a browser-provided leave-page warning when possible so you are reminded that unfinished work will remain available for resume. Browsers, especially on mobile devices, do not reliably let a web app run cleanup code as it closes. Use Discard & erase when you want the local resume copy removed immediately.

Basic usage analytics. Text-o-Matic uses Cloudflare Web Analytics to understand aggregate site use and performance. It does not receive imported contacts, merge-field values, contact-card fields, or message text, and Cloudflare Web Analytics does not use cookies or local storage for analytics.

Other local data. Text-o-Matic also stores non-list information such as preferences, usage counters, and recent column-mapping choices. Those records do not contain the recipient rows or prepared message text and may remain until browser site data is cleared.

Verify it yourself

Inspect Text-o-Matic's network traffic

You can use your browser's built-in developer tools to see every network request Text-o-Matic makes while you use it.

  1. Open Text-o-Matic, then open your browser's developer tools. In Chrome, Edge, and Firefox, F12 or Ctrl+Shift+I usually opens them.
  2. Select the Network tab and reload the page so you can see the requests made when the app starts.
  3. Paste or upload a test list, map its columns, and build a message while the Network tab remains open.
  4. You will see ordinary requests needed to load the site, its libraries, and anonymous analytics while online. What you should not see is Text-o-Matic transmitting your uploaded file contents, contact list, phone numbers, merge-field values, or message text.
  5. For an additional check, wait until the footer says Offline ready, disconnect from the internet, and continue using the app.
One important boundary: when you choose to open a prepared message, Text-o-Matic hands the recipient and message to the messaging app or service you selected. Network activity from that app or service is outside Text-o-Matic.

Device Transfer

How can a QR code work without uploading my list?

A QR code is just machine-readable data. It does not inherently mean “internet” or “website.” Text-o-Matic puts the transfer payload directly into the QR code instead of uploading your list and creating a server-side claim link.

Encryption is on by default. Before encrypted QR codes are created, Text-o-Matic encrypts the complete prepared transfer locally in your browser using AES-GCM. The QR codes contain only encrypted transfer data plus the information needed to assemble the parts.

The separate transfer code is the key. Text-o-Matic generates a transfer code made of six randomly selected short words on the first device. That code is not included in any QR code and stays hidden on the QR screen until you choose to reveal it. After all QR codes are scanned, reveal the code and type the six words into the receiving Text-o-Matic page so it can decrypt the list locally. Spaces or hyphens are both accepted.

This helps if another QR app keeps scan history. A camera or QR-scanning app can still retain the QR contents according to its own settings, but with encryption enabled those contents are ciphertext rather than readable names, phone numbers, messages, or contact-card fields. Keep the transfer code separate from the QR codes until the transfer is complete.

You can turn encryption off. Unencrypted transfer skips the transfer-code step, but the QR codes then contain readable prepared contact and message data. Treat unencrypted QR codes like the list itself, especially if the camera or QR-scanning app may retain scan history.

The code includes the Text-o-Matic web address so your camera can open the app. Transfer data is placed after the # in that address (the URL fragment). Browsers do not send URL fragments as part of the normal request for the page. Text-o-Matic also removes the transfer fragment from the visible receiving-page URL as soon as it reads it.

If the receiving device has not already cached Text-o-Matic, it may need internet access to load the app itself. That request is separate from transferring the list: Text-o-Matic does not upload a copy of the prepared list to store the transfer.

Multi-code transfers use temporary browser storage. Scanned parts are kept in IndexedDB until the transfer can be assembled. Encrypted parts remain encrypted there until you enter the transfer code. The temporary record is deleted when the transfer becomes the active texting queue; abandoned transfers expire after 24 hours of inactivity and are deleted the next time Text-o-Matic runs.

Easy way to think about encrypted transfer: the QR codes are locked envelopes carrying the data, while the separately displayed transfer code is the key. Neither one is uploaded to Text-o-Matic for storage.
